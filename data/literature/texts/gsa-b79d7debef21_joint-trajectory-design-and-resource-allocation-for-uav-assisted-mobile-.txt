J Wireless Com Network (2025) 2025:4 https://doi.org/10.1186/s13638-024-02426-9 EURASIP Journal on Wireless Communications and Networking Joint trajectory design and resource allocation for UAV-assisted mobile edge computing in power convergence network Junbin Cui1, Yong Wei1, Jianbo Wang1, Li Shang1 and Peng Lin2* Abstract In the power convergence network, a large number of intelligent power terminals (IPTs) are deployed, such as a variety of information collection terminals. Meanwhile, the unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) offer dependable services for IPTs in environments with minimal or no infrastructure and then combine with the mobile edge computing to realize low-latency task services. In this article, we consider a problem of computation offloading and UAV trajectory design to minimize the task offloading delay. Because the primal problem is non-convex, we first decompose it into two subproblems, i.e., joint computation offloading and resource allocation subproblem, and UAV trajectory design subproblem. For the first subproblem, we first reformulate it as a non-convex problem. And then, recognizing the problem’s high complexity, we choose to decompose it in a distributed form. Following this, leveraging the alternating direction method of multipliers, we introduce the joint computation offloading and resource allocation algorithm. For the second subproblem, we utilize the successive convex approximation method to solve this non-convex problem. Utilizing the solutions obtained from these two subproblems, we have introduced the joint computation offloading and UAV trajectory design (JCOUTD) algorithm to tackle the primal problem. The simulation results reveal that in comparison with other benchmark methods, the proposed JCOUTD algorithm displays enhanced performance in reducing total task offloading delay. Keywords: UAV trajectory design, Power convergence network, Mobile edge computing (MEC), Alternating direction method of multipliers (ADMM), Successive convex approximation (SCA) 1 Introduction The power convergence network is built on the foundation of an integrated, high-speed bidirectional communication network [1]. It achieves the goals of reliable, secure, costeffective, and safe grid through the applications of advanced sensing and measurement technology. In the power convergence network, a multitude of intelligent power termi nals (IPTs) are deployed, including temperature sensors, frequency sensors, and cam eras. These terminals generate numerous tasks that require computation processing [2]. This approach involves offloading tasks to potent cloud centers, streamlining their processing. However, the large offloading delay is presented due to the long distance between the cloud center and the IPTs [3]. To address this challenge, the concept of mobile edge computing (MEC) has been introduced [4]. Thanks to the close proximity of MEC servers to IPTs, task offloading delays can be significantly reduced. Considering the environment and cost constraints, there is limited or no communica tion infrastructure in some rural areas. In this situation, the unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs), known for their cost-effectiveness and exceptional mobility, have gained recog nition as an innovative solution for delivering dependable services to IPTs in environ ments with minimal or no infrastructure. Combining the UAVs and MEC can achieve the power convergence network goals of efficiency, intelligence, and reliability. Recently, the UAV-assisted computation offloading to minimize task offloading delay has been attracting significant attention. Considering the UAVs flying delay, data transmission delay, and task computation delay, a joint UAV trajectory design and computation resource allocation problem were formulated to minimize the task offloading delay. In order to solve this problem, a machine learning framework based on the Q-learning algorithm was proposed [5]. To minimize system delay, a problem was formulated that involves dynamic UAVs functioning as relays and static UAVs serving as fixed base stations (BSs). This challenge encompasses joint task allocation and dynamic UAV trajectory design. To address it, the deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithm was employed [6]. Considering the software defined network can improve the quality of services for users, a problem was framed that involves joint task offloading and resource allocation to minimize task processing delay. Addressing the non-convex nature of this problem, a solution was introduced in the form of a deep reinforcement learning (DRL)-based algo rithm [7]. In order to make UVA more suitable for power convergence network, papers [8, 9] proposed the index modulation technology, which allowed certain terminals to be idle and utilized the different on-off states to transmit information or task offloading. In practical engineering, there are a large number of IPTs deployed in the power conver gence network. The complexity of the task offloading algorithm escalates significantly with the growth in the number of IPTs and UAVs. However, the complexity of the existing algorithms is very high, especially for the DRL algorithms. Therefore, it is essential to introduce a low-complexity distributed task offloading algorithm within the power convergence network. Considering the shortcomings of existing literature, this paper proposes a joint com putation offloading and UAV trajectory design (JCOUTD) algorithm to minimize the task offloading delays within a power convergence network. The contributions of this paper mainly include the following aspects: • We formulate a problem that addresses the joint computation offloading and UAV trajectory design. This problem is structured with the objective of minimizing the total task offloading delay for IPTs, all the while taking into account factors such as task offloading delay, IPTs’ energy consumption, and system resource constraints. • Considering the coupling of optimization variables and the non-convexity of the formulated problem, the primal problem is decomposed into two subproblems. Finally, in Section 10, we draw our conclusions. 2 Related works Resource allocation and UAV trajectory design have recently garnered significant atten tion. These endeavors can be categorized into three groups based on their different objectives. The primary objective is to minimize energy consumption. Considering the fairness of the UAVs, a 3D multi-UAV trajectory design was formulated to reduce system energy consumption, where users select target UAVs for task computing. Utilizing the multiagent deep deterministic policy gradient (MADDPG) method, an optimal solution was successfully obtained [10]. A system energy consumption minimization problem was studied by jointly optimizing the beamforming vectors of UAVs, the computa tion resources of UAVs, the UAVs trajectory, and the transmission power of users. In order to solve the non-convex problem, a three stage iterative algorithm was proposed [11]. In order to extend the UAVs operating time, Wang et al. investigated the prob lem to minimize the energy consumption of UAVs. By decomposing the problem into two subproblems, an iterative algorithm was proposed to obtain the optimal solution [12]. Considering the high maneuverability of UAVs can enhance the communication and computation performance, a joint resource allocation, user association, and power control algorithm was proposed by utilizing the centralized multi-agent reinforcement learning method [13]. Du et al. formulated a problem of minimizing the UAV energy consumption by jointly optimizing the users association, computation resource allo cation, UAV hovering time, and wireless powering duration time. By transforming the problem into a convex problem and applying the flow-shop scheduling techniques, an alternative algorithm was proposed [14]. The second objective is to enhance computational efficiency. To maintain the sta bility of the dynamic task data queue and minimize secure computation require ments, a problem was formulated involving binary offloading decisions and resource allocation to maximize the computation efficiency. Through the application of the

[page 4]
Page 4 of 21Cui et al. J Wireless Com Network (2025) 2025:4 Dinkelbach method to render the problem more tractable, a novel online edge learn ing offloading algorithm was developed [15]. Considering the UAV-assisted relaying in the MEC networks, a computation efficiency maximization problem was formu lated by jointly optimizing the UAV trajectory, computation, and radio resources. By utilizing the Dinkelbach method, the block coordinate descent (BCD) method and the SCA technique, an algorithm was proposed [16]. Considering the limited system resources, finite UAV energy, and interference between the uplink and downlink, a joint computation allocation, UAV trajectory design, and radio resource alloca tion problem was formulated to maximize the computation efficiency. After that, a multi-stage alternative optimization algorithm was proposed [17]. Taking into account the potential for Internet of Things (IoT) nodes to utilize energy harvested from the UAV’s radio frequency, two optimization problems focusing on maximiz ing computation bits were framed, considering both partial and binary offloading scenarios. To tackle these issues, two iterative algorithms were devised for solving these different problems [18]. To optimize users’ computation bits while adhering to fairness constraints, a novel algorithm called soft actor-critic (SAC)-based UAV trajectory planning and resource allocation (SAC-TR) was introduced [19]. The third objective centers on minimizing task offloading delays. Zhang et al. har nessed the UAV’s dual roles as a computation node and relay node to devise a prob lem aimed at minimizing average user latency. To tackle this challenge, the problem was decomposed into three subproblems, and an approximation algorithm was pro posed for its resolution [20]. In order to meet the requirements of mobile broadband communication (eMBB) and massive machine-type communication (mMTC) users, an optimization problem to minimize the task offloading delay was formulated. And then, the authors proposed the Rosen’s gradient projection algorithm to solve this problem [21]. Han et al. introduced a problem aimed at minimizing the average task offloading delay by jointly optimizing user association and UAV deployment. By uti lizing the optimal transport theory and classical particle swarm optimization algo rithm, the problem was solved [22]. Hu et al. devised a problem with the objective of minimizing the maximum delay, achieved by jointly optimizing computation off loading, computation resource allocation, and UAV trajectory. To address this chal lenge, the authors introduced a novel penalty dual decomposition-based algorithm [23]. To minimize task offloading delay, a problem involving the joint optimization of UAV placement, resource allocation, and computation offloading was formulated. An algorithm utilizing the double deep Q-Learning (DDQN) and deep deterministic policy gradient (DDPG) algorithms was introduced to obtain a near-optimal solu tion [24]. In practical engineering, there are a large number of IPTs deployed in the power convergence network. As the number of IPTs and UAVs increases, the complexity of the task offloading algorithm will escalate significantly, especially for the centralized algorithms and DRL algorithms in the existing works. Therefore, taking into account practical considerations, it is essential to develop a distributed task offloading algo rithm with low complexity for the power convergence network.

Given that the problem is non-convex, it is necessary to break it down into two subproblems: one addressing joint computation offloading and resource allocation and the other tackling UAV trajectory design. For the first subproblem, we first utilize variable relaxation and variable substitution to reformulate it as a non-convex problem. And then, the problem is approached in a distributed manner using the ADMM method. This leads to the introduction of the JCORA algorithm based on the derived solution. Meanwhile, for the second subproblem, the non-convex issue is addressed using the SCA method. According to the solutions of two subproblems, we propose the JCOUTD algorithm. Data size is meas ured in KB, and computation resources are measured in Megacycles. The maximum delay tolerance is 0.3 s. The UAV operates at an altitude of 20 m with a maximum speed of 20 m/s. Each period has a duration of 10 s, and each time slot lasts for 0.5 s. To assess the performance of the proposed JCOUTD algorithm, we conduct com parisons with the following three algorithms.
